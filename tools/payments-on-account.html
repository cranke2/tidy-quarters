Payments on account calculator

If your Self Assessment bill is £1,000 or more, HMRC usually asks you to pay half of next year's tax in advance, in January and July. In your first year this can mean paying 150% of your bill in one go. Type your figures to see what's due. It's filled in with an example.

How it works

  • You make payments on account unless last year's bill was under £1,000, or more than 80% of your tax was already collected at source.
  • Each payment is half of last year's bill, due 31 January and 31 July.
  • The balancing payment on 31 January is whatever is left after the payments you already made. Student loan repayments and capital gains tax go in the balancing payment.
  • Expecting a lower bill this year? You can ask HMRC to reduce your payments on account online, or with form SA303. If you reduce them too far, you'll pay interest on the shortfall.
  • From April 2029, Budget 2026 plans to replace payments on account with automated in-year payments, such as Direct Debit. The details come next year. Read more.

Source: gov.uk, "Understanding your Self Assessment tax bill: payments on account", checked October 2026.
