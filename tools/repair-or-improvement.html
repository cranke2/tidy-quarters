The rules in plain English

  • A repair restores something to its original condition, sometimes by replacing parts. Replacing storm-damaged roof tiles or redecorating between tenants are repairs.
  • The nearest modern equivalent still counts as a repair when the improvement is incidental, such as double glazing in place of single glazing.
  • Capital costs can't be claimed against rent. Examples are an extension, a higher-spec kitchen, or works to make a derelict property fit to let. Keep the receipts, because they may reduce Capital Gains Tax when you sell.
  • Replacement of domestic items relief covers replacing movable items such as furniture, carpets, appliances and kitchenware. It doesn't cover the first purchase. If the new item is an upgrade, you claim only what a like-for-like replacement would have cost. Add delivery and disposal costs, and take off anything you got for the old item. A part-exchange discount simply lowers the cost.
  • Fixtures aren't domestic items. Baths, toilets, built-in wardrobes, boilers and radiators fall under the repair rules instead.
  • The relief doesn't apply if you use Rent a Room relief, or if you claimed capital allowances on the item.
