Do I need Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

Updated October 2026 · 4 minute read

If you're self-employed or a landlord, and your income from those adds up to more than £30,000 a year, you will very likely have to use Making Tax Digital (MTD) from 6 April 2027. It's based on your income before expenses, not your profit.

HMRC estimates about 1.08 million people join in April 2027 and another 975,000 in April 2028.

What counts as qualifying income

Add together your gross income (turnover) from:

  • all your self-employments, and
  • UK property, plus foreign property if you're UK resident.

Leave out wages from a job (PAYE), dividends, and your share of a partnership.

Three quick examples

Priya, hairdresser: £36,000 of takings, £14,000 of costs. Profit is £22,000, but turnover is over £30,000, so Priya starts in April 2027.

Dan, landlord with two flats: £19,200 rent a year. Dan is under £20,000, so MTD isn't currently required.

Sam, plumber and landlord: £24,000 of fees plus £9,000 of rent is £33,000. Sam starts in April 2027.

What it means in practice

  • Keep your income and expense records digitally, in software or a spreadsheet.
  • Send a short quarterly update to HMRC four times a year. The first is due by 7 August 2027.
  • Send a final declaration by 31 January 2029, which replaces your tax return.

A spreadsheet is allowed if you send it to HMRC through bridging software. You don't have to pay for a monthly accounting subscription.

Can I be exempt?

If you can't use digital tools because of age, disability, where you live or religious reasons, you can apply to HMRC for an exemption. Search gov.uk for "apply for an exemption from Making Tax Digital for Income Tax".
