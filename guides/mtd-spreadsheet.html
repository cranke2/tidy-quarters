Can I use a spreadsheet for Making Tax Digital?

Updated October 2026 · 5 minute read

Yes. HMRC accepts spreadsheets for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, as long as your figures reach HMRC digitally. In practice that means using bridging software, which reads the totals from your spreadsheet and sends them for you.

How bridging software works

  1. You keep your income and expenses in Excel, Google Sheets or LibreOffice.
  2. Each quarter, the bridging software reads the cells with your totals.
  3. It shows you the figures, and you press submit.

What you can't do is copy figures by hand from your spreadsheet into another program. That breaks the "digital link" HMRC requires. To find bridging software, search gov.uk for "find software that works with Making Tax Digital for Income Tax" and filter for bridging software. Some options are free for simple cases.

What your spreadsheet needs

  • A row for every transaction, with the date, amount and a category.
  • HMRC's categories for expenses. If your turnover is under £90,000 you can send a single expenses total, but categories still help you and your accountant.
  • Cumulative totals. Each quarterly update covers the start of the tax year to the end of that quarter, not just three months.
  • Separate records for each business and for property income.

Common mistakes

  • Recording your own drawings as an expense.
  • Claiming 100% of a phone or car you also use personally. Record the business share.
  • Including loan capital repayments. Only the interest is an expense.
  • Dating income by invoice in one place and by payment in another. Pick one method and stick to it.
