Budget 2026: what it means for sole traders and landlords

Updated 31 October 2026 · 4 minute read

The Chancellor delivered Budget 2026 on 28 October 2026. Here are the changes that matter if you're self-employed, have a side income or let out property. Most take effect from 6 April 2027.

1. The trading allowance triples to £3,000

From the 2027-28 tax year, the trading allowance rises from £1,000 to £3,000. The Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading and miscellaneous income rises to £3,000 too. So if you sell crafts, do odd jobs or freelance on the side, you won't need to register until your income before costs goes over £3,000.

Until 5 April 2027, the old £1,000 limit still applies. The Budget papers don't mention any change to the £1,000 property allowance. Check where you stand now with the side income checker.

2. Landlords pay more on rental profit from April 2027

From 6 April 2027, property income gets its own tax rates. Budget 2025 first set these at 22%, 42% and 47%. The Budget 2026 rates table now shows them two points higher.

Rate2026-272027-28
Basic20%24%
Higher40%44%
Additional45%49%

Rental profit is taxed after wages and self-employment profit, and before savings and dividends. Your personal allowance is used against your other income first. Budget 2025 said the separate property rates apply in England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Example. A basic-rate landlord with £10,000 of rental profit pays £2,000 on it in 2026-27. In 2027-28 the same profit costs £2,400.

Mortgage interest. You'll still get a tax credit instead of a deduction. Budget 2025 said the credit would be given at the property basic rate from 2027-28. The Budget 2026 papers don't state the new figure, so we'll update this page when HMRC confirms it.

3. Income tax bands and allowances stay frozen

  • The personal allowance stays at £12,570, and the basic rate band at £37,700. The higher rate band runs to £125,140 in 2027-28.
  • Class 4 National Insurance is unchanged: 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above that.
  • The Class 2 small profits threshold rises to £7,355 for 2027-28. Voluntary Class 2 rises to £3.80 a week.
  • Savings income also moves to 24%, 44% and 49% from April 2027. Dividend rates are unchanged.

To see your 2026-27 bill, use the self-employed tax calculator.

4. Paying tax during the year, from April 2029

After a consultation this summer, the government decided that from April 2029, people who make payments on account will pay through automated in-year payments, such as Direct Debit, by default. People with enough PAYE income can choose to pay through their tax code instead. How often payments are taken, and the safeguards, will be set out next year. Nothing changes for January 2027 or July 2027: see the payments on account calculator.

5. Letting agents may report your rent to HMRC

HMRC is consulting until 20 January 2027 on letting agents and tenancy deposit schemes reporting property income data to HMRC every month or quarter. It would have no minimum threshold. If it goes ahead, HMRC will see more of what landlords earn, so it's worth getting your records in order.

What hasn't changed

The Budget papers we read don't change the Making Tax Digital timetable. Over £30,000 starts in April 2027, and over £20,000 in April 2028. Your next quarterly update is due on 7 November. Not sure if you're in? Try the MTD checker.
