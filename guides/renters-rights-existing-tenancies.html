Tenancies that started before 1 May 2026: what changed

Updated October 2026 · 3 minute read · England

The Renters' Rights Act applies to existing tenancies too, not just new ones. Most assured shorthold tenancies became periodic assured tenancies on 1 May 2026.

The information sheet

Every existing tenant had to be given the government's Renters' Rights Act Information Sheet by 31 May 2026 (later only in a few cases where a section 21 or section 8 notice was already served). If you missed it, give it now. The penalty is up to £7,000, and up to £40,000 if the failure continues after a first penalty.

Rent increases

Rent can now only be raised through the section 13 process, which has longer notice periods than before. A section 13 notice served before 1 May 2026 follows the old rules. Rent review clauses in old tenancy agreements no longer work for increases.

Rent in advance

If an existing tenancy already collects rent in advance, that can carry on until the tenancy ends. For new tenancies, you can't demand more than one month's rent at a time.

Still the same

Deposits still go in a government-approved scheme within 30 days with the prescribed information, and gas safety, EICR and EPC rules still apply.

Sources: gov.uk's Guide to the Renters' Rights Act, and the NRLA's guide to existing tenancies.
